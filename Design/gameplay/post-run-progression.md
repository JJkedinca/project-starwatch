# Post-Run Progression

## Purpose

Post-run progression is what persists between entirely separate runs. It should expand the player's possible strategies without turning victory into a grind.

The core terminology is:

- **Run:** One complete campaign / new game, targeted at roughly six to eight hours.
- **Timeline:** One empire attempt within a run.
- **Rewind:** Resetting the current timeline and moving to the next attempt.
- **Rewinds 0–4:** The five total timelines in one run.
- **Post-run progression:** Unlocks and upgrades that remain available when starting a new run.

Within a run, timeline power resets after a rewind while knowledge and selected permanent unlocks carry forward. Post-run progression is the longer-term layer that expands what future runs can offer.

## Goals

Post-run progression should:

- Give players a reason to replay after completing or losing a run.
- Expand the number of viable strategies.
- Make new combinations possible.
- Reward meaningful discoveries and accomplishments.
- Add optional challenge for experienced players.

It should not:

- Require hours of grinding before a new player can win.
- Reduce every run to the same strongest permanent build.
- Replace discovery and planning with flat stat accumulation.
- Make the base game feel incomplete without high-tier unlocks.

The guiding principle is:

> A new run should give the player more possibilities, not simply a larger number.

## Persistent Unlock Categories

### Races and civilizations

Unlockable races or civilizations can change the shape of a run through different:

- Starting economies.
- Resource affinities.
- Research priorities.
- Diplomatic strengths.
- Environmental tolerances.
- Fleet philosophies.

They should be asymmetric rather than strictly better. One race may make nuclear fleets easier, while another may excel at diplomacy, exploration, or defensive infrastructure.

### Ships and technologies

Post-run unlocks can add:

- New ship classes.
- New weapons and defensive systems.
- New fleet doctrines.
- New research branches.
- New production methods.
- New ways to interact with alien technology.

These should add options to the run's decision space. Unlocking a ship does not guarantee that its resources or supporting technology will be available in every timeline.

### Artifacts and modifiers

Artifacts and modifiers can alter how a run works rather than simply increase starting strength.

Examples:

- Begin with a clue toward a relic's location.
- Start with improved anomaly scanning.
- Make a particular diplomatic contact more accessible.
- Change how a resource is converted into fleet power.
- Alter the behavior or timing of a minor invasion phase.
- Add a special starting doctrine or empire rule.
- Make a specific discovery more likely to appear.

These should create new build opportunities and unusual constraints. They are most interesting when they combine with the current map and available resources.

### Power-up points

Players may earn power-up points from completed runs, major discoveries, campaign objectives, or other meaningful accomplishments. Points can be spent on a permanent progression tree or unlock system.

Good uses include:

- Unlocking new races, ships, technologies, artifacts, or modifiers.
- Adding another starting option.
- Improving access to a research branch.
- Providing a modest research, scanning, or construction bonus.
- Expanding the number of available doctrines or starting choices.

Permanent numerical bonuses should remain modest. Power-up points should improve flexibility and consistency without making later runs win automatically.

### Difficulty levels

Additional difficulty levels should provide a reason to replay a solved campaign. Difficulty should change the strategic problem, not only inflate enemy health and damage.

Possible difficulty changes include:

- More aggressive neighboring factions.
- Scarcer resources or fewer safe expansion routes.
- Less warning before invasion events.
- More demanding diplomatic conditions.
- More dangerous or competitive relic locations.
- New enemy fleet behaviors.
- Tighter escalation deadlines.
- Additional invasion objectives or simultaneous fronts.

Higher difficulties can also introduce new discoveries, modifiers, or alternate defense plans so that they are more than endurance tests.

## What Should Persist Between Runs?

This remains an open design decision. The following layers should be considered separately:

### Post-run unlocks

Races, ships, technologies, artifacts, modifiers, power-up investments, and difficulty levels should persist between runs once unlocked.

### Run-specific knowledge

Exact relic locations, invasion routes, faction secrets, and event triggers may be specific to one run's setup. If they persist forever, later runs may become solved checklists. If they reset completely, the player may feel that earlier discoveries had no lasting value.

Possible approaches:

- Preserve only the unlock or general lesson, while generating a new location or setup.
- Preserve the archive but use different invasion scenarios for later runs.
- Let players choose between a known scenario and a fresh scenario.
- Carry forward broad knowledge while resetting exact coordinates, timing, or resource availability.

This should be decided alongside map generation and scenario structure.

### Player knowledge

Regardless of what the game formally preserves, the player's own understanding should remain meaningful. The ideal experience is that a player recognizes familiar patterns while still needing to analyze the current setup.

## Rewarding Failed Runs

Post-run rewards should not require victory only. A failed run can still grant:

- Discovered technologies.
- Recovered relic data.
- Alien fleet intelligence.
- Faction information.
- Completed objectives.
- Power-up points.
- New starting options.

Rewards should reflect meaningful accomplishments rather than elapsed time. Voluntarily rewinding early should remain convenient, but it should forfeit discoveries that the player could have obtained by continuing the timeline.

## Relationship to Run-Specific Combos

Post-run progression expands the ingredients available across future runs. The current timeline determines which ingredients can actually be combined.

For example:

- A permanent nuclear doctrine unlocks the possibility of nuclear fleets.
- The current run still needs uranium, a suitable reactor relic, and enough production capacity.
- If uranium is unavailable, the player pivots to another plan rather than forcing the nuclear build.

This preserves both long-term progression and run-specific optimization:

- **Between runs:** unlock more tools and possible strategies.
- **Within a run:** identify which tools fit the current setup.
- **Across timelines:** use discoveries to secure and optimize the chosen plan.

## Design Principles

1. Post-run progression should expand possibilities more than it adds raw power.
2. New content should be asymmetrical and create different openings.
3. A fresh player should be able to win without extensive grinding.
4. Permanent bonuses should improve flexibility, not erase strategic risk.
5. Difficulty levels should introduce new strategic pressures and opportunities.
6. Failed runs should reward meaningful discoveries and objectives.
7. The current run's resources and setup should determine which combos are possible.
8. The player should never need to use the same strategy every run.
9. Exact knowledge persistence should be balanced against the risk of turning future runs into checklists.

## Open Questions

- Which unlocks are earned by victory, discovery, objectives, or power-up points?
- Do races represent whole civilizations, starting doctrines, or both?
- How many permanent numerical bonuses are healthy before progression becomes grind?
- Should new ship classes enter the available pool immediately or require a discovery during a run?
- Are artifacts selected before a run, discovered during it, or both?
- Which run-specific facts persist between separate runs?
- Are there multiple campaign scenarios, and can players choose a known or unknown scenario?
- Do higher difficulties remix content or mainly add new constraints?
- What is the maximum post-run content breadth needed before the game feels complete?
