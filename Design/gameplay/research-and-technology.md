# Research and Technology

## Role in the Game

Research converts discoveries, resources, diplomacy, and observations into tools the player can use to prepare for the invasion.

Research should:

- Create meaningful run-specific strategies.
- Reward exploration and persistent knowledge.
- Provide new capabilities rather than only larger numbers.
- Support fleet, economy, travel, diplomacy, and defense.
- Force choices without making a run unwinnable.

The player should not follow the same technology order every run. The generated map, available resources, discovered relics, alien contacts, enemy weaknesses, and research opportunities should determine which branches are most attractive.

## Research Structure

Research should use a hybrid model:

- An authored prerequisite graph provides logical progression.
- Eligible research opportunities are randomized within that graph.
- Some branches remain hidden until the player discovers a lead.
- The player chooses which available project to pursue.

This is not a card or tactics system. Randomization changes which research projects are available; it does not make research itself a separate tactical game.

Prerequisites should prevent nonsensical combinations and provide understandable progression without forcing a single universal build order. A technology may require a previous technology, a resource, a building, a faction relationship, or a discovery.

## Research Offers and Prerequisites

The player should see three eligible research projects at a time. Options are not deferred into a permanent queue; when a project is selected or completed, the available choices refresh according to the current research pool.

The player initially researches one project at a time. Research infrastructure or other upgrades may later increase the number of active projects and/or the number of projects offered at once. These are separate ways to improve research throughput and choice.

Prerequisite depth can vary:

- Basic technologies may require no prerequisite or one simple prerequisite.
- Specialized technologies may require one or two related technologies.
- Advanced technologies may require longer chains, cross-domain requirements, or multiple discoveries.
- Alien or rule-breaking technologies may require research plus diplomacy, relics, events, or enemy analysis.

When a player discovers a lead, the related technology enters the eligible pool with a higher selection weight while still respecting its prerequisites. The exact treatment of especially important leads remains a balance question.

## Technology Availability

Technologies should fall into several availability levels.

### Core technologies

Core technologies are available in every run so the player can function:

- Basic construction and colony support.
- Basic research and energy production.
- Foundational FTL.
- Basic fleet production.
- Basic defenses.
- Basic ship roles.
- Generic ways to damage shields, armor, and hull.

Core technologies may still have meaningful upgrades, but missing them should not make a scenario unwinnable.

### Specialized technologies

Specialized technologies are available through the randomized research pool once their prerequisites are met. They may not appear in every run or may appear in different combinations.

Examples include:

- Advanced carrier systems.
- Missile saturation weapons.
- Nuclear propulsion.
- Specialized shield types.
- Advanced armor-piercing weapons.
- Long-range artillery systems.
- Repair and logistics improvements.
- Alternate classes of existing ships.

Specialized options should create strong directions without being the only possible response to a threat.

### Discovery-gated technologies

Some powerful or unusual technologies should remain hidden until the player discovers a lead. The lead may come from:

- A relic or ancient ruin.
- A special event.
- Enemy wreckage or combat analysis.
- Alien diplomacy or language research.
- A sabotage discovery.
- A planet-specific anomaly.
- A blueprint.
- A species or faction trait.

The discovery may reveal a research path rather than granting the technology immediately. Once revealed, the path still requires prerequisites, research time, resources, and implementation.

Discovery-gated technologies should generally be optional rule-bending tools rather than mandatory requirements for victory.

## Sources of Technology

Different sources should have different design roles:

- **Normal research:** Provides reliable specialization and improvements to known systems.
- **Blueprints:** Reveal new named ship classes, buildings, or production methods that must usually be researched before use.
- **Relics:** Provide immediate effects or unusual technologies that bend normal rules.
- **Events:** Reveal temporary opportunities, research leads, or unique projects.
- **Alien diplomacy:** Opens technologies, joint projects, or research paths unavailable through ordinary research.
- **Enemy wreckage:** Reveals weapons, defenses, technologies, and countermeasures.
- **Anomalies and ruins:** Provide access to ancient or otherwise hidden branches.
- **Species or faction identity:** Biases the available pool or provides unique starting options.

The same effect should not need to be available from every source. A normal research project may improve shield regeneration, while a relic may create a weapon that partially bypasses shields.

## Faction Keywords

Player civilizations and major alien factions can have one or two signature keywords that influence their research opportunities, ship classes, and fleet synergies.

Possible keywords include:

- **Stealth:** Reduced detection, ambush bonuses, decoys, and sensor disruption.
- **Drain:** Shield theft, energy disruption, repair denial, and resource leeching.
- **Swarm:** Cheap ships, strike-craft capacity, and saturation attacks.
- **Fortress:** Armor, defensive stations, repairs, and system control.
- **Precision:** Sensors, sniper classes, targeting, and long-range damage.
- **Regeneration:** Shield recharge, field repairs, and sustained operations.
- **Disruption:** Engine slowing, weapon suppression, and retreat interference.
- **Salvage:** Debris recovery, reverse engineering, and wreck-based research.

Keywords can affect:

- Starting technologies.
- Research weighting.
- Available named ship classes.
- Faction-specific relics or blueprints.
- The strength of related fleet effects.
- Which discoveries create especially powerful combinations.

Keywords should provide affinity and unique opportunities rather than hard-locking the player out of basic counters. A stealth-focused civilization can still research armor, while a fortress-focused civilization can still use carriers; their keyword simply makes certain strategies more efficient and distinctive.

External alien factions can have their own keywords. Diplomacy, reverse engineering, or an alliance may give the player access to a limited version of another faction's specialty, creating another reason to seek out specific factions during a run.

### Discovering Faction Specialties

Some major factions should be playable civilizations, while others should remain non-playable minor factions. Both types can contribute to the research and discovery game.

Playable major factions may have:

- A distinctive starting keyword or keyword combination.
- Unique starting technologies or research weighting.
- Faction-specific ship classes, relics, or production methods.
- A different preferred route through the research pool.
- Strengths and weaknesses that change which discoveries are most valuable.

Minor factions do not need a complete playable technology set. They can instead have one or two distinctive mechanics that make encountering them worthwhile:

- A stealth faction reveals sensor disruption or ambush technology.
- A drain faction provides shield-siphoning research.
- A salvage faction teaches more efficient debris recovery.
- A fortress faction reveals defensive station or armor techniques.
- A navigation faction offers route, relay, or travel technology.

The player may gain access to a minor faction's specialty through diplomacy, alliance, reverse engineering, espionage, captured infrastructure, or recovered wreckage. The result should usually be a limited research branch, blueprint, or mechanic rather than the full faction identity.

Discovering a faction in one timeline should make its location, keyword, and access requirements available in the run archive. Later timelines can prioritize the faction and pursue the most efficient interaction, while still paying the research, diplomatic, production, or military cost of turning that knowledge into usable technology.

## Discoveries, Research, and Implementation

A discovery often follows a multi-step process:

1. **Discover:** Find a relic, blueprint, alien device, wreckage sample, or research lead.
2. **Understand:** Translate, analyze, or reverse-engineer the discovery.
3. **Research:** Complete the required project and prerequisites.
4. **Implement:** Rebuild the infrastructure, production, or fleet needed to use it.

For example:

> Find a Longbow-class battleship blueprint → research its design → unlock the class → construct it at a suitable shipyard.

Not every discovery needs the full sequence:

- An active relic may provide an effect immediately.
- A strategic observation may only update the intelligence archive.
- A blueprint usually reveals a class or production path.
- Enemy wreckage may reveal a counter-tech project.

This creates an opportunity cost. Finding something powerful does not mean it can be used immediately; the player must decide whether to invest time and resources into converting knowledge into power.

## Research Infrastructure and Pacing

Research infrastructure can support tall development:

- Each research lab level contributes research rate to a global empire research pool.
- Higher-level labs provide disproportionately more research than a level-one lab.
- Research infrastructure upgrades may support parallel projects, increase offered options, or reduce research time.
- A dedicated research world can become a valuable strategic anchor.

However, research building levels should not create a long mandatory ladder before the player can access meaningful technology. A player should not need to repeatedly upgrade a research building through many levels before reaching the first useful branch.

Technology chains should be relatively shallow. A progression such as Warp I → Warp II → Warp III can work when each tier provides a meaningful new capability:

- Warp I enables basic expansion and travel.
- Warp II improves reach or opens difficult regions.
- Warp III enables extreme-distance opportunities or advanced ships.

The player should not be expected to complete every branch in one timeline. A 1.5-hour timeline should contain a few meaningful research decisions, while the full run reveals and exploits deeper options. The exact number and duration of projects require balance testing.

Research projects are one-way commitments in the initial design. Players cannot pause, abandon, or redirect an active project for now. The consequences of committing to the wrong project can be tuned later if this proves too restrictive.

## Research Across Rewinds

Research persistence should be separated into knowledge and implementation.

Knowledge that should generally persist within a run includes:

- Discovered research leads.
- Known prerequisites.
- Revealed research branches.
- Blueprint locations and requirements.
- Completed run-specific technology knowledge.
- Discovered alien technology and enemy weaknesses.

Timeline power that resets includes:

- Incomplete research project progress.
- Research buildings and infrastructure.
- Current resources and production.
- Active fleet upgrades.
- Existing ships and installed technology.

For now, completed research stays fully unlocked and available across rewinds within the same run. The civilization retains the knowledge, while the physical infrastructure, resources, ships, and production required to exploit it still reset. The exact balance between immediate use and any future reactivation cost remains open for balance testing.

Future timelines should not require the player to rediscover every foundational technology. However, the player still needs to rebuild the infrastructure, resources, shipyards, and fleets required to exploit advanced technology.

For example:

> Rewind 0 discovers the requirements for Warp II but cannot complete them.  
> Rewind 1 begins with the Warp II path known and reaches it earlier.  
> Rewind 2 builds the economy and research infrastructure around using Warp II.

This allows technology to progress across the full five-timeline campaign without carrying the previous empire's physical power forward.

## Research Between Separate Runs

Research unlocks are run-specific for the initial design. Completed technologies, discovered research branches, and faction specialties do not carry into a separate run.

Post-run progression may still add new races, ships, artifacts, modifiers, or difficulty options, but it does not preserve the current run's research tree or completed technologies.

## Research and Run-Specific Builds

Research should convert discoveries into coherent military and economic identities:

- Heavy uranium worlds plus nuclear research create high-endurance fleets.
- Alien language research enables negotiations or joint fleets with friendly species.
- Enemy wreckage reveals a countermeasure branch against a dangerous ship class.
- A carrier blueprint plus carrier research creates a strike-craft-focused fleet.
- A rare shipyard plus industrial technology makes an unusual class affordable.
- A relic plus shield research creates an unconventional defensive fleet.
- FTL research plus a hard-to-reach region opens unique rewards.

These combinations should be powerful enough to support a player power fantasy, but conditional on the current run's resources and discoveries. The player should be able to ignore one opportunity and pursue another viable plan.

## Multiplayer Research

Each player should have an independent research queue and research progress:

- Players do not share research progress.
- Players do not automatically share completed technologies.
- Each empire must pay its own research, infrastructure, and implementation costs.
- Species traits and research choices remain part of each empire's identity.

Discoveries can be more cooperative than research. A blueprint or research lead discovered by one player may be added to the shared run archive so all players know that it exists and where it was found. Each player may still need to research and implement it independently.

Blueprints are owned by the player who discovers them for the initial design. They are not automatically shared or unlocked for other players. Trading, copying, or diplomatic transfer may be added later.

Physical relics and unique artifacts should generally remain with the player who discovers or recovers them unless explicit transfer rules are introduced.

## Fairness and Randomization Rules

Randomized research should create adaptation, not dead ends:

- Core capabilities must always be available.
- Every run should expose viable paths for economy, fleet power, defense, and travel.
- Basic responses to common enemy defenses should not depend on one hidden discovery.
- Advanced counters can be more efficient, cheaper, or more powerful than baseline solutions.
- Discovery-gated technology should provide alternate plans rather than mandatory answers.
- Unselected research should not necessarily disappear forever, but the player should have meaningful opportunity costs when pursuing it.
- Research options should reflect the current map, resources, factions, and discoveries without making outcomes fully predictable.

## Open Questions

- What global research rate and lab scaling feel appropriate?
- How many active projects and offered options can upgrades support?
- How heavily should discovered leads be weighted in the pool?
- How many hidden technologies should a typical run contain?
- How many meaningful projects can fit into each timeline?
- Should any completed technologies later require reactivation at higher Ascension levels?
- Can blueprint trading or duplication be added without removing the value of discovery?
