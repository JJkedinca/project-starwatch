# Multiplayer Plan

## Core Model

Starwatch supports one to four players cooperating against the invasion.

Each player controls a separate fledgling empire. Players own their territory, economy, research, diplomacy, fleets, and local discoveries. The invasion is shared, and the players must form a coalition strong enough to survive it.

The multiplayer goal is:

> Build four independent empires that are individually viable but collectively interdependent.

Players should not feel like administrators of one shared empire. They should feel like leaders of separate civilizations that need one another to survive.

## Shared Campaign Structure

Multiplayer uses the same campaign structure as single-player:

- One shared run lasts approximately six to eight hours.
- A run contains five total timelines: Rewinds 0–4.
- Timeline power resets for every player after a rewind.
- The rewind budget is shared by the group.
- Discoveries are recorded immediately in a shared archive.
- The final victory condition is shared.

Voluntary rewinds should be proposed to the group and confirmed collectively. One player should not be able to accidentally end the current timeline for everyone else.

## What Each Player Controls

Each player owns:

- Their own systems, colonies, and territory.
- Their own resource income and construction queues.
- Their own research and technology choices.
- Their own ship designs and fleets.
- Their own diplomatic relationships.
- Their own relics and local discoveries.

Players can offer support, trade resources, share intel, and coordinate fleets, but should not directly control another player's empire.

Player responsibilities should emerge from geography and the current situation rather than rigid classes. One player may focus on research in one timeline and military expansion in another.

## What the Coalition Shares

The coalition shares:

- The invasion threat and escalation clock.
- The intelligence archive.
- Major enemy weaknesses and observed behavior.
- Finite strategic resources on the map.
- Coalition projects and final objectives.
- The rewind budget.
- The overall victory and defeat state.

The coalition should not have one universal treasury for normal empire operations. Separate economies preserve the identity and decision-making of each player. Shared resources should usually mean resources that are scarce across the map and must be traded, protected, or strategically allocated.

## Cooperation Drivers

### Finite strategic resources

Important resources should be distributed across player territories or contested systems:

- One player controls the uranium region.
- Another discovers nuclear research.
- A third owns the shipyard that can build the required fleet.
- A fourth protects the supply route from the invasion.

No player can complete the entire plan alone, but every player retains ownership of their empire.

Resources should be valuable because they enable different plans, not because every player must receive an equal share. Trading and coalition agreements should be meaningful strategic choices.

### Asymmetric starting positions

Players should begin with different local advantages and problems:

- A resource-rich but exposed frontier.
- A safe research region with limited industry.
- A diplomatic position near friendly aliens.
- A central chokepoint with strong defensive value.

Each position should offer a viable path to power while creating a reason to depend on the others.

### Multiple simultaneous fronts

The invasion should eventually create more threats than one player can handle:

- One empire holds the invasion route.
- Another protects the research network.
- Another manages hostile neighboring aliens.
- Another prepares a staging area for the final offensive.

Losing one front should strengthen or redirect the invasion elsewhere, making the situation a coalition problem rather than four separate single-player games.

### Coordinated operations

Some objectives should reward or require synchronized action:

- Destroy several relays within a timing window.
- Hold a jump gate while another fleet attacks behind it.
- Escort a science expedition through hostile space.
- Bait an invasion fleet into a system while another player sabotages its supply network.
- Combine fleets for a decisive auto-resolving engagement.

The fleet battle itself remains autonomous. Cooperation happens through preparation, movement, timing, composition, and commitment.

### Coalition projects

Some powerful options should require contributions from multiple empires:

- A weapon that needs research from one empire and rare materials from another.
- A translation network that requires diplomatic contact from several species.
- A relay sabotage operation that needs fleets at multiple locations.
- A final offensive that requires staging bases controlled by different players.

Coalition projects should produce major advantages without becoming mandatory for every possible defense plan.

### Shared information

Discoveries should enter the shared archive immediately:

- Relic locations.
- Invasion routes and timing.
- Enemy fleet weaknesses.
- Faction incentives and betrayals.
- Sabotage points.
- Anomaly triggers.

Different players can discover information in different regions, creating a reason to communicate and connect clues. Critical information should be visible in the archive after discovery; cooperation should come from interpreting and acting on it, not from hiding essential facts from teammates.

## Difficulty Scaling

Multiplayer difficulty should scale through strategic breadth and coordination pressure rather than simply multiplying enemy health or damage.

With more players, increase some combination of:

- Number of active invasion fronts.
- Number of concurrent objectives.
- Resource scarcity and contested locations.
- Diplomatic pressure.
- Enemy fleet coordination.
- Timing-sensitive operations.
- Consequences when one empire collapses.

One player should manage a smaller number of simultaneous threats. Four players should face a larger crisis that requires specialization, negotiation, and coordinated movement.

The goal is not for four players to fight one enemy fleet that is four times stronger. The goal is for four players to realize that four separate problems must be solved at nearly the same time.

## Defeat and Player Elimination

A player losing a colony or fleet should weaken the coalition without necessarily ending the run immediately. This creates rescue objectives and allows the remaining players to respond.

Possible consequences include:

- Loss of a unique resource.
- A new invasion route opening.
- Refugees or fleets needing support.
- A temporary reduction in research or production.
- A player operating from a smaller emergency capital.

Complete player elimination and its effect on the run remains an open design question. The game should avoid making one early mistake remove a friend's ability to participate for the remaining hours.

## Solo Compatibility

The same systems should work with one player controlling one empire:

- Solo receives fewer simultaneous fronts and coalition dependencies.
- The player has full control of their own empire.
- Some coalition projects can be completed through allied AI or reduced requirements.
- Multiplayer should add coordination load, not make solo feel like managing four empires.

Four-player co-op should provide more strategic breadth, not require the game to be four times slower or four times more complicated to operate.

## Preventing One Player from Carrying the Team

Every empire should have meaningful strengths and responsibilities. The strongest player should not be able to solve the entire invasion alone.

Useful safeguards include:

- Rare resources distributed across different regions.
- Objectives that require multiple locations.
- Enemy fronts that punish over-concentration.
- Coalition projects with multiple contribution types.
- Distinct but equally viable empire openings.
- Shared consequences when one player is ignored.

These should create interdependence without forcing rigid player classes or preventing a player from recovering after a mistake.

## Multiplayer Design Principles

1. Each player controls a real empire, not a role inside one empire.
2. Separate economies preserve ownership and meaningful decisions.
3. Scarce map resources create negotiation and trade.
4. The shared invasion creates the common objective.
5. Cooperation should come from dependencies, timing, and simultaneous threats.
6. Fleet combat remains auto-resolving; coordination happens before and around battles.
7. Discoveries are shared immediately through the archive.
8. A player's defeat should create a crisis before it creates elimination.
9. More players should increase strategic breadth, not only enemy statistics.
10. Solo should remain a complete experience.

## Open Questions

- Should players be able to trade systems, or only resources and fleet support?
- Can allied fleets merge into one controlled task force, or must each player retain control?
- How are joint operations confirmed and coordinated in the interface?
- Should diplomacy between player empires be formal treaties or informal agreements?
- What happens if a player disconnects during a timeline?
- Can a player voluntarily leave their empire to an allied AI?
- What conditions constitute coalition defeat?
- How much information should be shared automatically versus requiring explicit communication?
- Should each player have a different rewind milestone or should the group share one campaign progression?
